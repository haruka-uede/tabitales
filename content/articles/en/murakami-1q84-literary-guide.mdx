*1Q84* (2009–10) opens with Aomame stuck in a taxi on the Metropolitan Expressway, and does something few novels do: it sends its heroine down a real emergency staircase to escape — and in doing so, into a subtly altered version of 1984 Tokyo. That staircase isn't invented. It exists, exactly where the novel says it does, and has become one of the most reliably visited real-world locations tied to any Murakami book.

*1Q84* is also unusually well mapped compared to some of Murakami's more diffuse settings. Fans and pilgrimage sites have tracked down real addresses behind Tengo's Koenji neighborhood, the Azabu garden widely thought to have inspired the wealthy dowager's "Willow House," and even the small Chiba towns tied to Aomame and Tengo's childhoods. This guide covers the staircase itself plus the other real Tokyo locations worth building a day (or two) of visits around.

## Stop 1: The emergency staircase, Shuto Expressway Route 3

Aomame's taxi is stuck on the elevated Shibuya Route (3号渋谷線) near Sangenjaya, and she climbs down an emergency staircase near the Ikejiri exit to reach Route 246 below. The staircase — narrow, exposed steel, spiraling down the support column — is real infrastructure, built for expressway maintenance and emergencies rather than sightseeing, but it's clearly visible from street level, and fans have marked it with "1Q84" graffiti over the years.

- **Getting there**: Sangenjaya Station or Ikejiri-Ohashi Station (both Tokyu Denentoshi Line), then walk toward the Route 246/Metropolitan Expressway junction between the two. The staircase is visible from the street below — you can't climb it (it's for expressway emergency use only), but the view from ground level is the point.
- **Good to know**: This is a real piece of expressway infrastructure in an ordinary Tokyo neighborhood, not a tourist attraction — there's no signage, and it's easy to walk past without knowing what you're looking at. Have a photo of the scene from the book or film adaptation on hand for reference.

## Stop 2: Koenji Chuo Park — where Tengo watches two moons

Tengo lives in Koenji, a quiet, walkable residential and shopping neighborhood in Suginami, for most of the novel's second half — and it's in Koenji Chuo Park (高円寺中央公園), a small neighborhood park a few minutes from the station, that he first notices a second moon hanging in the sky: the clearest sign that he, too, has slipped into the same altered world as Aomame. Aomame herself later moves to Koenji as well, making the neighborhood one of the few places in the novel both protagonists actually inhabit.

- **Getting there**: JR Chuo Line to Koenji Station; the park is about a 3-minute walk from the south exit, at 4-31-7 Koenji-Minami, Suginami.
- **Good to know**: This is an ordinary neighborhood park, not a monument — nothing on site marks its connection to the novel. Koenji's real draw is its retro shopping arcades and secondhand clothing shops, easy to combine with the park visit.

## Stop 3: Arisugawa-no-miya Memorial Park and Zenpukuji Temple, Azabu — the real "Willow House"

The novel's wealthy dowager lives in a mansion called the Willow House, in Azabu, one of Tokyo's most expensive residential districts. No single address is confirmed as the model, but fans and pilgrimage guides consistently point to Arisugawa-no-miya Memorial Park (有栖川宮記念公園) — a former Imperial family estate turned public park in Minami-Azabu — as the closest real match for the setting's atmosphere. Nearby Zenpukuji Temple (麻布山善福寺) adds a literal namesake connection: its approach holds the "Willow Well" (柳の井戸), tied to a temple founding legend. The temple's actual signature tree, though, is a roughly 750-year-old ginkgo rather than a willow, so don't expect a dramatic willow tree on site.

- **Getting there**: Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line to Hiroo Station, about 3–5 minutes' walk to the park.
- **Good to know**: Both sites are free, outdoor, and open at all hours. Arisugawa Park has a pond, hillside paths, and a public library on its grounds — worth a visit even without the *1Q84* connection.

## In the novel: further-flung locations

A handful of other real places round out *1Q84*'s geography — more for background color than as dedicated stops:

- **Shinjuku**: The historic Nakamuraya building (新宿中村屋), near Shinjuku Sanchome, and the Kinokuniya main store across from it both still operate today and appear in the novel's Tokyo.
- **The Okura Tokyo, Toranomon**: An important later scene is set at what the novel calls Hotel Okura. The hotel has since been rebuilt, reopening in 2019 as The Okura Tokyo — the physical building has changed since Murakami wrote the novel.
- **Ichikawa, Chiba**: The real hometown of both Aomame and Tengo, a commuter city just across the prefectural border from Tokyo.
- **Chikura, Chiba**: A real town on the Boso Peninsula that inspired the novel's "Town of Cats," where Tengo's father lives out his final days in a sanatorium.
- **Futamatao, Ome City**: A remote station at Tokyo's western edge, near the mountain trailhead area where Fuka-Eri stays with Professor Ebisuno.

## Practical travel notes

- **Best time to visit**: Any time — Sangenjaya, Koenji, and Azabu are all street-level city visits, not seasonal ones.
- **Base yourself in**: central Tokyo. All three main stops sit within about 20–30 minutes of most central neighborhoods on their respective train lines, and are close enough to each other to combine two in a single day.
- **If you only have time for one stop**: the Sangenjaya staircase — it's the most visually striking, the most reliably identified with the novel, and tied directly to *1Q84*'s opening pages.
- **The book itself**: the standard English translation (Books 1–2 by Jay Rubin, Book 3 by Philip Gabriel) is published by Knopf/Vintage.
