Shikoku's two largest cities, Matsuyama and Takamatsu, sit at opposite ends of the island and roughly a century apart in the novels tied to them — Soseki's 1906 comic novel *Botchan* in Matsuyama, and Murakami Haruki's 2002 *Kafka on the Shore* in Takamatsu.

The JR Ishizuchi limited express connects them directly in about two and a half hours, making this an easy island-crossing route for readers of either book, or both.

## Stop 1: Dogo Onsen, Matsuyama — the bathhouse behind *Botchan*

<StopImage src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/57/Dogo_Onsen_Honkan_%28Main_building%29%2C_%E9%81%93%E5%BE%8C%E6%B8%A9%E6%B3%89_%E6%9C%AC%E9%A4%A8_-_panoramio.jpg" alt="Dogo Onsen Honkan, Matsuyama" credit="z tanuki" creditUrl="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dogo_Onsen_Honkan_(Main_building),_道後温泉_本館_-_panoramio.jpg" />

Soseki taught English in Matsuyama for one unhappy year in 1895, and turned it into *Botchan*, a short comic novel about a hot-tempered young teacher clashing with everyone around him. The novel barely disguises Dogo Onsen Honkan, Japan's oldest hot spring bathhouse still in operation, under the fig-leaf name "Sumita no Onsen" — a wooden sign inside still warns "Botchan, no swimming," a nod to a scene in the book.

Matsuyama has embraced the connection city-wide, down to a replica steam tram and an animated clock outside the bathhouse. Full stop-by-stop details: [Following *Botchan* to Matsuyama](/articles/soseki-botchan-literary-guide).

## Stop 2: Takamatsu and Sakaide — the library at the heart of *Kafka on the Shore*

<StopImage src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Kamada_Foundation_Museum_2021-08_ac_%281%29.jpg" alt="The Kamada Kyosaikai Kyodo Museum, Sakaide" credit="Asturio Cantabrio" creditUrl="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kamada_Foundation_Museum_2021-08_ac_(1).jpg" />

Murakami's *Kafka on the Shore* follows a teenage runaway to a private library in Takamatsu — one Murakami has said outright he invented: "the Komura Memorial Library certainly exists — that's a lie. It doesn't exist. I made it up myself."

That hasn't stopped readers from proposing real candidates, and the most-cited one isn't in Takamatsu itself but in neighboring Sakaide: the Kamada Kyosaikai Kyodo Museum, a 1922 building with the same donor-founded, art-collecting profile as the novel's fictional library. Full details: [Following *Kafka on the Shore* to Takamatsu](/articles/murakami-kafka-on-the-shore-literary-guide).

## Practical travel notes

- **Getting between stops**: The JR Ishizuchi limited express runs directly between Takamatsu and Matsuyama in roughly 2.5 hours, no transfer required. Sakaide is a short local-train detour from Takamatsu, about 20–30 minutes each way.
- **Order**: Either direction works. Starting in Matsuyama lets you end the trip in Takamatsu, which connects onward to Honshu via the JR Marine Liner to Okayama's Shinkansen line.
- **Best time to visit**: Spring and autumn avoid Shikoku's hottest, most humid stretch for both cities.
- **Base yourself in**: One night in Matsuyama (Dogo Onsen itself is the most convenient base) and one in Takamatsu if taking this at an easy pace, though it's also doable as a very long single day.
